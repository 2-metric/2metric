01 / Keep these close

A few words go a long way.

Choose a situation and learn two or three phrases first. The notes tell you when to use them. These are written examples for Brazil; listen to a Brazilian Portuguese speaker as well to practise the sounds and rhythm.

01 · Greetings

Oi! / Olá!

Hi! / Hello!

A simple way to begin. Follow it with a smile and your question.

02 · Greetings

Bom dia!

Good morning!

Use it when greeting someone in the morning, including in a shop or café.

03 · Greetings

Boa tarde!

Good afternoon!

An afternoon greeting you can use before asking for directions.

04 · Courtesy

Por favor.

Please.

Add it to a request: “Um café, por favor.”

05 · Courtesy

Obrigado. / Obrigada.

Thank you.

Conventionally, a male speaker says obrigado and a female speaker says obrigada. The ending refers to the speaker.

06 · Courtesy

Com licença.

Excuse me.

Useful when passing someone or politely getting their attention.

07 · At a café

O cardápio, por favor.

The menu, please.

A short request for the food or drinks menu.

08 · At a café

Um café, por favor.

A coffee, please.

The server may ask which kind or size. Pointing to the menu can help.

09 · At a café

Uma água sem gás, por favor.

A still water, please.

Sem gás means still; com gás means sparkling.

10 · At a café

O que você recomenda?

What do you recommend?

A way to invite a suggestion. Be ready to ask the speaker to repeat it.

11 · At a café

A conta, por favor.

The bill, please.

Use it when you have finished and would like to pay.

12 · At a café

Vocês aceitam cartão?

Do you accept cards?

Ask before ordering if you need to pay by card.

13 · Getting around

Onde fica o banheiro?

Where is the bathroom?

Begin with com licença when approaching someone.

14 · Getting around

Onde fica o ponto de ônibus?

Where is the bus stop?

Save the written name of your destination so you can show it if needed.

15 · Getting around

É perto daqui?

Is it near here?

Use this after naming the place you want to reach.

16 · Getting around

Quanto custa?

How much does it cost?

If you miss the number, ask the person to write it down.

17 · Getting around

Tenho uma reserva.

I have a reservation.

Useful at accommodation or a restaurant. Have the booking name ready.

18 · Getting around

Pode me mostrar no mapa?

Can you show me on the map?

Helpful when a spoken set of directions is difficult to follow.

19 · Understanding

Estou aprendendo português.

I’m learning Portuguese.

Let the person know you are practising and may need a little time.

20 · Understanding

Não entendi.

I didn’t understand.

Use it when you missed what someone just said.

21 · Understanding

Pode repetir mais devagar?

Can you repeat that more slowly?

Learn this early. You can ask for help with the conversation itself.

22 · Understanding

Você fala inglês?

Do you speak English?

An option when you need to explain something beyond your current Portuguese.

23 · Understanding

Pode escrever, por favor?

Can you write it down, please?

Useful for a price, an address or a word you do not recognise.

24 · Asking for help

Você pode me ajudar?

Can you help me?

Follow it with your question, a written address or the item you need help with.

No matching phrases. Try a shorter word or choose “All situations”.

02 / Put the words together

Your first café conversation.

Practise both parts, then cover the Portuguese and try the customer’s lines from memory. This is one possible exchange; real replies will vary.

Before your trip, practise the answers as well as the questions. Asking for a menu is one small task; understanding a follow-up question is another.

A coffee to go

Cliente: Bom dia! Um café, por favor.
Customer: Good morning! A coffee, please.
Atendente: Para tomar aqui ou para levar?
Server: To drink here or to take away?
Cliente: Para levar, por favor.
Customer: To take away, please.
Atendente: Mais alguma coisa?
Server: Anything else?
Cliente: Só isso. Obrigado!
Customer: That’s all. Thank you! Use obrigada if that is the form you use.

03 / Before you go

Make five phrases yours.

You do not need to memorise the whole page. Start with the situations you expect on your first day and keep a printed copy nearby.

  1. Pick five phrases. Choose one greeting, one request, one question and two ways to ask for clarification.
  2. Say them out loud. Use a recording by a Brazilian Portuguese speaker to check pronunciation, then repeat at a comfortable pace.
  3. Change one detail. Swap a coffee for a water, or a bathroom for a bus stop. Keep the rest of the phrase familiar.
  4. Rehearse a reply. Ask a partner to answer unexpectedly, then practise asking them to repeat.

Words worth recognising in a reply

Sim / Não
Yes / No
Aqui / Ali
Here / There
À direita / À esquerda
To the right / To the left
Perto / Longe
Near / Far

Written phrases cannot show every sound. Avoid relying only on English-style pronunciation spellings; hearing and repeating the Portuguese is part of the practice.

Before you pack.

Is this phrasebook for Brazil or Portugal?

It is written for travel in Brazil. Some phrases are also used in Portugal, but vocabulary, pronunciation and everyday usage can differ. Choose listening practice that matches your destination.

Can I use the guide without an internet connection?

Yes. Save this complete HTML file to your device or print it before travelling. The phrasebook and search do not need an internet connection; the link to lessons opens an external website.

Will knowing these phrases make me conversational?

This is a starting set for short interactions, not a fluency course. Practising listening, likely replies and ways to ask for clarification helps you use the phrases more flexibly.
